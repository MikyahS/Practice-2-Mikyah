The Bauhaus

Bauhaus was a German art and design school founded in 1919. It combined art, architecture, and craftsmanship, focusing on simplicity, functionality, and modern design.

Bauhaus design is known for clean lines, geometric shapes, minimal decoration, and the idea that everyday objects should be both beautiful and useful.

One school, three cities, fourteen years

The Bauhaus was founded in 1919 in Weimar, Germany, by architect Walter Gropius. It was created after World War I during a time when artists and designers wanted to create a new, more modern style. The school later moved to Dessau in 1925 and then to Berlin in 1932 before closing in 1933.

The Bauhaus attracted young artists, designers, architects, and craftspeople from Germany and other countries. Students learned through hands-on workshops in areas such as woodworking, metalworking, weaving, pottery, and typography. The school encouraged students to experiment, work together, and develop new ideas instead of simply copying traditional art.

Walter Gropius wanted the Bauhaus to bring together art, crafts, and architecture. His goal was to create useful and affordable designs that could improve everyday life. Instead of separating artists from craftspeople, the Bauhaus believed they should work together to create simple, functional, and beautiful designs for the modern world.

What came out of the workshops

Wassily Chair

The Wassily Chair, designed by Marcel Breuer, was made from steel tubes and leather. Its simple, lightweight design showed the Bauhaus idea that furniture should be both functional and modern.

Bauhaus Lamps

Bauhaus workshops created many simple, geometric lamps, including designs by Wilhelm Wagenfeld. These lamps used materials such as glass and metal and focused on practical lighting with a clean, modern appearance.

Bauhaus Textiles

The weaving workshop produced colorful rugs, wall hangings, and fabrics. Students experimented with geometric patterns and new materials, helping create a modern style of textile design.

Bauhaus Tea Infuser

The Bauhaus tea infuser, designed by Marianne Brandt, was made from metal and shaped with simple geometric forms. It was designed to be practical and easy to use while also looking modern. It is a good example of how Bauhaus designers combined function, simplicity, and beauty.

How it ended, and why it did not

The Bauhaus officially closed in 1933 in Berlin. The school was forced to shut down after pressure from the Nazi government, which opposed its modern and experimental ideas.

Before closing, the Bauhaus had already moved from Weimar to Dessau and then Berlin because of political pressure. After the school closed, many teachers and students left Germany and moved to other countries, especially the United States, where they continued spreading Bauhaus ideas and influencing modern architecture, art, and design.
